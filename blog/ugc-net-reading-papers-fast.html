What UGC NET Prep Taught Me About Reading Papers Fast

Iqbal K I · April 2026 · 4 min read

Preparing for the UGC NET in Commerce and Management meant covering an enormous, heterogeneous syllabus in a fixed amount of time — financial management sitting next to research methodology sitting next to economic theory, none of it optional. The only way through was building a habit I didn't recognize as transferable until years later, deep into a PhD: reading for structure before reading for content.

Structure first, content second

Exam prep forces you to ask, before reading a single paragraph closely, what kind of claim a section is making — definition, method, result, critique. That habit turned out to map almost exactly onto how I now approach a new paper. Before reading the introduction closely, I skim the abstract, the section headers, and every figure caption. That pass alone usually tells me whether the paper's core contribution is theoretical, empirical, or methodological, which changes how carefully I need to read the rest.

Knowing what to skip is a skill

You cannot deeply read everything on a NET syllabus in the time available, and you cannot deeply read every paper cited in a literature review either. What exam prep taught me is that skipping isn't a failure of rigor — it's a resource allocation decision, and making it consciously beats making it by default (i.e., reading whatever's first and running out of time for what's last). I now triage a stack of papers the same way I used to triage a syllabus: skim all of them for relevance first, then allocate deep-reading time by how central each one is to the question I'm actually asking.

Recall under pressure is a different skill than understanding

The NET format rewards being able to retrieve a fact quickly under time pressure, which is a genuinely different skill from understanding a concept deeply — and conflating the two was a mistake I made early in prep. I've carried the distinction into research: I now separate "do I understand this method" from "can I explain this method to a committee in ninety seconds," and treat them as two things I need to practice independently, not one thing that follows automatically from the other.

The exam taught me how to move fast without being careless. Research is mostly the same skill, applied to a syllabus nobody wrote down.

I qualified UGC NET & JRF in Commerce and NET in Management before starting the PhD, and I genuinely didn't expect either to still be useful for reading methodology sections years later. They are.
